# Computational methods and acquisition records

Samples were supplied by Yibin Jinxilai Liquor Industry Co., Ltd. and described by the author as strong-aroma Baijiu sampled in July 2026. There are 139 independent bottles. The author reports a 1 mm cuvette, ambient temperature maintained within 20 to 25 degrees Celsius, and three technical measurements averaged per bottle for each spectral modality. Raman was measured with an ACCUMAN portable instrument from Ocean Optics, USA; UV-visible with a T600 instrument from Beijing Purkinje General Instrument Co., Ltd., China; NIR with an N70 Fourier-transform instrument from Hanon Advanced Technology Group Co., Ltd., China. A temperature range is not a recorded stability tolerance. Instrument scan counts, resolution, optical settings and native axis units remain to be checked against records.

The author describes GC-MS using a DB-WAX column 60 m by 0.25 mm by 0.25 micrometre, helium at 1.0 mL/min, injector at 230 degrees Celsius, 50:1 split and 1 microlitre injection. The stated oven program is 40 degrees Celsius for 1 minute, 10 degrees Celsius/min to 180 degrees Celsius for 5 minutes, then 10 degrees Celsius/min to 280 degrees Celsius for 5 minutes. The exact column product and its upper-temperature rating require confirmation; the recorded method is not silently changed. EI energy was 70 eV, source temperature 280 degrees Celsius, transfer-line temperature 260 degrees Celsius, full scan m/z 20 to 500, and library matching used NIST 05a.L. The stated internal standards were n-pentyl acetate, tert-amyl alcohol and 2-ethylbutyric acid for esters, alcohols and acids, respectively. Three reference measurements were averaged. GC-MS model, standard confirmation, calibration functions, internal-standard concentrations, reference quality control, precision and concentration reporting basis still require laboratory records. No invented validation data are included.

The core benchmark uses 3 repeats of 5 outer bottle folds with 3-fold inner validation. Model-specific preprocessing and tuning are selected within the training data. Seven fixed modality masks and target-specific and shared-panel selection rules are evaluated for PLS, Ridge, RBF-KRR and single-response sequential orthogonalized PLS. UV retains a raw candidate; Raman raw, SNV and asymmetric-baseline-plus-SNV candidates are compared; NIR raw, SNV and first-derivative candidates are compared. Nonuniform coordinate interpolation is a numerical treatment; optical resolution is not inferred. No spectral cropping, automatic bottle deletion or water/ethanol subtraction is performed.

Shared panels average all eleven inner standardized target losses before choosing one acquisition mask. The instrument requirement is the union of modalities required for all eleven outputs. Inner tolerances 0, 2, 5 and 10 percent are sensitivity settings. They are not validated analytical noninferiority margins.

Learning tasks compare nested 40- and 70-bottle fit subsets with the full outer training size of 111 or 112 bottles, keeping test identities fixed. Common-scale macro evaluation fixes the full outer-training SD across these sizes; that assessment weight is not used in fitting. Five bootstrap refits per outer task describe sensitivity to training composition; duplicate copies of a bottle are kept in one inner fold. Three unconditional NIR bottle permutations provide a diagnostic of alignment information for PLS, Ridge and KRR only.

All work reuses one previously inspected cohort. Nested splits provide matched internal comparisons but do not erase earlier redevelopment or constitute external, cross-day, cross-batch or cross-producer validation. Negative results are part of the evidence.
